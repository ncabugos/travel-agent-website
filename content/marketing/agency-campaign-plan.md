That is good news. It means the fix is building the machine, not discounting the product.

## 3. Who we are targeting, and the Fora / Cadence reality

**Primary ICP (where the money is):** boutique-to-midsize luxury agencies, roughly 2 to 50 advisors, often Virtuoso members, whose website badly underserves their brand. Decision-maker is the owner, principal, or managing director. These are real buyers for your Custom and Agency tiers.

**Secondary ICP:** larger agencies and small host agencies that want to offer branded sites to their members. This is the Agency tier, and it is a partnership conversation.

**The Fora and Cadence note you need to hear.** Both are host agencies that build their own advisor technology in-house. Fora's entire pitch is its proprietary advisor platform. Cold-selling either of them a website product will not land, and it can make EAH look like it does not understand the market. Keep them on the list, but in a **partnership / integration track**, not the sell-a-site track. The right opening with them is "where could EAH complement what you already give advisors," not "buy our websites."

The most valuable thing those big names give you is not their business. It is their **advisor rosters as a prospecting map.** Advisors leave hosts constantly. HostAgencyReviews exists precisely because advisors shop hosts. That is your hunting ground.

## 4. Pricing review, with structural recommendations

Your current structure:

| Tier | Monthly | Setup |
|---|---|---|
| Starter | $89 | $499 |
| Growth | $179 | $1,499 |
| Custom | $349 | $2,999 |
| Agency | from $899 | from $4,999 |

Plus your founding offer: first 30 days free.

**The real friction is the setup fee.** Ask a boutique owner who has never heard of EAH to pay $1,499 to $2,999 up front, on faith, and most will stop reading. The monthly prices are fine. They are an on-brand floor and they signal seriousness, which your positioning wants. The setup fee is what kills a cold conversion before trust exists.

My recommendations, structural:

1. **Waive setup for a Founding Cohort.** First 10 to 15 agencies, setup fee dropped to $0, framed as founding membership and not a discount. This is consistent with your brand brief, which says pricing is a floor and not a sale. Pair it with the 30-day trial you already run. This single change removes the biggest barrier in your outreach.
2. **Reframe the setup fee as "white-glove migration, included."** For the founding cohort, the build is a value you give, not a toll you charge. Reintroduce it as a paid line item later, once you have testimonials and a waitlist.
3. **Add annual plans (pay for 10 months, get 12).** Improves cash flow, increases commitment, and lowers churn. Most SaaS conversions at this level prefer an annual option once they trust you.
4. **Make Agency pricing legible.** "From $899" is vague to a buyer. Move to a clear base platform fee plus a per-advisor seat, and lead with the things only Agency gives: the agent directory, multi-advisor management, and unified billing.
5. **Do not cut monthly prices.** Compete on proof and service, not on being cheap. Cheap repels the exact buyer your brand brief is built to attract.

**The advisor caveat:** every pricing change is a hypothesis until your funnel produces real conversion numbers. Right now you have none, so we cannot know if price is a barrier. Ship the founding-cohort setup waiver because it removes obvious friction, then let the first 90 days of real pipeline tell us whether price needs more surgery.

## 5. The organic lead-gen engine (no paid spend)

Six channels, all feeding one HubSpot pipeline.

**A. SEO blog (owned authority).** Publish advisor-facing articles targeting commercial-intent keywords that agency owners actually search. You already have one strong asset drafted (the hotel programs piece). This is the top of the funnel and the thing that compounds.

**B. Lead magnet (the capture mechanism).** One gated, genuinely useful asset, for example "The Independent Advisor's Website Checklist: 27 things a luxury client notices before they call you." Reader gives an email to download. That email flows straight into HubSpot and triggers nurture. This is the single missing piece that turns readers into contacts.

**C. Email nurture (automation).** An automated sequence fires after a magnet download. The same engine runs your warm outbound to the cold agency list. Everything logged.

**D. Agency landing page (the conversion destination).** A dedicated page built to convert agency owners, separate from your homepage. It is where every email and every content CTA points. Copy drafted in the assets file.

**E. LinkedIn, founder-led (distribution).** Agency owners live on LinkedIn, not TikTok. Repurpose each blog post into 2 to 3 posts. Founder voice, your voice, beats brand-account voice every time at this stage.

**F. Community and directory presence (trust).** Get EAH listed and reviewed where advisors research vendors, answer questions in advisor Facebook groups and forums, and pursue a HostAgencyReviews presence. Organic, slow, high-trust.

**Automation backbone:** HubSpot workflows connect it: form submission, list enrollment, sequence send, and a task for you to personally follow up on anyone who replies or books. The website forms must be wired into HubSpot so leads and outreach finally live in one place.

## 8. 90-day roadmap

**Phase 1, weeks 1 to 2 (build the machine):** wire website forms into HubSpot; set up the pipeline stages; publish the agency landing page; finalize and gate the lead magnet; ship the offer one-pager; approve the founding-cohort setup waiver.

**Phase 2, weeks 3 to 6 (turn it on):** launch warm outbound to the first 50 to 100 boutique agencies in batches; publish one blog post per week; post on LinkedIn three times per week; nurture sequence live.

**Phase 3, weeks 7 to 12 (measure and double down):** review reply rate, consults booked, trials started, and conversions; publish the Eden case study; run the first real pricing test now that there is a funnel to test on.

**KPIs to watch:** outbound reply rate, consults booked, trials started, trial-to-paid conversion. Vanity metrics (impressions, followers) come last.
